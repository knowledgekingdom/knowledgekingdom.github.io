Several tempting claims require revision. 'Columbus discovered American agriculture' ignores millennia of Indigenous domestication. 'The two continents had no contact of any kind before 1492' ignores earlier contacts and complex Pacific evidence. 'All Native Americans had no immunity to any disease' misunderstands immunology. 'Disease alone conquered the Americas' obscures choices and institutions. 'The exchange was balanced' mistakes a descriptive label for an ethical description.

Another misconception imagines biological transformations happening at the same speed everywhere. Crop adoption took years or centuries; outbreaks and colonization reached different regions at different times. A final misconception turns a historical model into unquestionable fact: estimates of pre-contact population or climate consequences should be presented with their uncertainties. \[<a href="/028-the-columbian-exchange/sources/#source-16" class="cite">16</a>\]\[<a href="/028-the-columbian-exchange/sources/#source-19" class="cite">19</a>\]\[<a href="/028-the-columbian-exchange/sources/#source-25" class="cite">25</a>\]

Strong history neither sensationalizes nor sanitizes. It identifies what evidence can establish, what remains debated and why the questions matter to our understanding of the modern world.
