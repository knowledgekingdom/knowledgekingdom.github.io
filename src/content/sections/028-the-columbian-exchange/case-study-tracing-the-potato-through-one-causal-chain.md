Start with Andean domestication. Farmers cultivated diverse potatoes suited to different conditions. Spanish colonial contact created routes by which some potatoes reached Atlantic islands and Europe. Slow experimentation allowed the crop to establish itself in suitable regions. In certain places, it supported higher yields and population growth. In others, adoption remained limited for cultural or environmental reasons. \[<a href="/028-the-columbian-exchange/sources/#source-8" class="cite">8</a>\]\[<a href="/028-the-columbian-exchange/sources/#source-10" class="cite">10</a>\]

The next chain is less cheerful. In the nineteenth century, susceptibility to disease, widespread dependence among poor rural households and inadequate or harmful political responses helped turn a crop failure in Ireland into mass famine and migration. That catastrophe was not an unavoidable property of potatoes. It arose from the interaction of plant disease with an unequal human system.

Trace each step, then identify where an alternative choice or condition might have changed the result. This is an example of history as causal reasoning rather than memorizing the date a potato entered Europe.
