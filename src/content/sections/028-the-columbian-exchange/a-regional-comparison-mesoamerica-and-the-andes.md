In Mesoamerica, maize and its associated agricultural traditions had long provided food, ritual meaning and political resources. Colonial society introduced wheat, livestock and new demands on land while epidemics and labor regimes caused immense disruption. Indigenous farmers did not necessarily cease growing familiar crops; many adapted multiple systems and kept distinctive practices alive.

Andean highlands presented different conditions. The potato and other tubers had deep local histories, while llamas and alpacas supplied transport, fiber and other resources. European livestock and crops entered heterogeneous environments, with outcomes shaped by altitude and local policies. Mining centers and colonial labor obligations altered the movement of people and food, sometimes pulling resources away from communities.

Neither comparison should suggest that all communities experienced identical outcomes. The analytical gain comes from recognizing variation. The hemisphere-wide framework highlights connection; regional history explains how it operated on the ground.
