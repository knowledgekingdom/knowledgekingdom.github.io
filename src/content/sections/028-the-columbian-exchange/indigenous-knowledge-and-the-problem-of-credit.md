The global success of maize, potatoes, cassava and cacao depended on generations of Indigenous experimentation and domestication. Their botanical traits were not gifts discovered in an untouched landscape by arriving Europeans. Farmers selected seeds and varieties, learned storage, developed cultivation practices and passed expertise through families and communities. Those innovations predated global commercial fame.

A historical narrative that credits a European explorer with 'bringing civilization' through a plant often confuses transportation with creation. The sailor could carry the crop because someone already knew how to grow it. Even in colonial settings, Indigenous labor and guidance frequently sustained agricultural expansion, though archives might record the colonizer's name more prominently.

Recognizing origins does not prohibit later cultures from making genuine contributions. Chinese, African and European farmers bred new varieties and invented new recipes. Knowledge Kingdom's aim is to track each contribution carefully rather than assign ownership to the most powerful participant in a chain of exchange.
