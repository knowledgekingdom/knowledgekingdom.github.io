Many societies in the Americas experienced urban contraction or major political transformation before Europeans arrived. Teotihuacan's central institutions changed, Classic Maya dynasties in various southern lowland cities ended, and political authority shifted among Andean states. Cahokia's population and influence declined. Scholars investigate environmental variability, conflict, resource management, changes in trade and legitimacy, demographic processes and local political choices. The balance differs for each case. A drought model cannot be imported unchanged into an entirely different time and ecosystem.

'Collapse' is a dangerous shorthand. It may refer to a court no longer commissioning inscriptions, a settlement losing residents, a road system failing, an elite lineage ending or a region's political center shifting. These events are not identical. If people moved and reorganized, then the older city's decline is not the disappearance of the society. Maya languages survived; Pueblo descendants inhabit the Southwest; Andean communities adapted imperial institutions and continued their traditions. An entire hemisphere did not wait motionless between the end of an ancient dynasty and European contact.

Students should ask for specific evidence whenever an explanation sounds too neat. What exactly changed? In what dates? Which valleys or cities? What environmental indicators support the proposal? Does a series of violent depictions show general war or court rhetoric? Does a paleoclimate record from a lake establish conditions for every nearby settlement? The best explanations compare several causes and admit where the current evidence remains thin.
