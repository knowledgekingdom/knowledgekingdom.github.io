Serbia opposed Bulgaria's sudden enlargement, and war broke out in November 1885. Bulgarian troops defeated Serbian forces in the battles around Slivnitsa and advanced westward before the conflict was halted. Austria-Hungary's intervention to protect Serbia from a more serious defeat underscored the limits of purely bilateral military outcomes. The 1886 peace returned to the prewar frontier between Serbia and Bulgaria, even while the Bulgarian union survived.

Why did Serbia attack? Explanations involve the perceived regional balance, King Milan's political calculations, concerns about prestige, and the assumption that the Bulgarian army would be unable to respond effectively while forces were concentrated elsewhere. Avoid saying that the war proves an eternal hostility between Bulgarians and Serbs. Governments make decisions under particular domestic pressures; soldiers and civilians pay the costs; later national memories can turn a contingent conflict into an apparently timeless feud.

The war also showed the relationship between diplomacy and military capacity. Bulgarian success strengthened the political survival of Unification, but it did not resolve questions about Macedonia, future borders or external patrons.
