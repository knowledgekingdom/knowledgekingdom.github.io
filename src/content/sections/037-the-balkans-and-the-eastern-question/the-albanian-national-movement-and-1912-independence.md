The League of Prizren of 1878 had responded to fears of territorial partition, but Albanian nationalist movements developed through changing coalitions and aims in the following decades. Language education, publications and political organizations strengthened an Albanian national program across communities divided by religion and regional interests. Relations with Ottoman reformers fluctuated; demands for local autonomy and representation sometimes collided with programs of stronger central control.

Uprisings in Albanian-populated areas during the years around 1910–1912 added pressure to the weakened Ottoman state. In November 1912, as armies of the Balkan League advanced, an Albanian assembly at Vlorë proclaimed independence. Recognition and boundaries were then affected by great-power negotiations, including the 1913 London discussions. Significant Albanian-speaking populations were left outside the new state's borders, producing further grievances.

It would be misleading to present Albanian independence solely as the product of one battle or one external sponsorship. It combined internal organization, fears about regional partition, imperial crisis and international diplomacy.
