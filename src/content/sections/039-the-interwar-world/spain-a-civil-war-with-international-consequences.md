Spain's Second Republic, established in 1931, confronted demands for land reform, church–state change, regional autonomy, and military restructuring. Polarization grew amid political violence and disputes over the direction of reform. A military rebellion in July 1936 failed to seize the whole country and escalated into the Spanish Civil War, lasting until 1939. Francisco Franco became the leading figure of the insurgent Nationalist coalition, while the Republic included liberals, socialists, communists, anarchists and other groups with significant disagreements. \[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-29" class="cite">29</a>\]

Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy aided the insurgents; the Soviet Union supported the Republic, and international volunteers joined the International Brigades. The bombing of Guernica in 1937 became a symbol of violence against civilians, but the war's atrocities and political repression were not confined to one incident. The Republic fell in 1939, followed by Franco's dictatorship. Foreign intervention and non-intervention policies helped determine the conflict's military balance. It was not simply a dress rehearsal for World War II; its specifically Spanish social struggles and the lives of its victims deserve independent attention. \[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-29" class="cite">29</a>\]
