In India, Gandhi and other anticolonial leaders organized non-cooperation and later civil-disobedience campaigns against British rule. The Salt March of 1930 challenged a familiar colonial monopoly and turned an everyday commodity into a political symbol. Indian independence politics were diverse, including arguments over representation, religion, class, gender, and the future constitutional structure. In Africa, labor struggles, religious associations, pan-African debates, and nationalist organizations developed under varying colonial systems. Global depression intensified many of these tensions. \[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-15" class="cite">15</a>\]\[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-32" class="cite">32</a>\]

Latin American governments confronted collapsing export markets and, in some places, military interventions or new state-led development policies. Brazil's political transformation after 1930 under Getúlio Vargas combined state-building, labor initiatives, nationalism, and increasingly authoritarian government. Mexico's revolutionary aftermath produced major institutional and land-policy changes, including reforms during Lázaro Cárdenas's presidency. These histories cannot be folded neatly into a single European ideological triangle. The interwar world also contained competing movements for land, labor rights, democracy, independence, and social reform outside the major powers. \[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-2" class="cite">2</a>\]\[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-32" class="cite">32</a>\]
