The contradictions of the settlement were especially visible in colonized societies. Indian activists had supported the Allied war effort, while British rule continued and political unrest intensified. The Amritsar massacre in 1919 became a symbol of colonial coercion. In Egypt, nationalist protests in 1919 preceded a limited form of independence in 1922, although British influence and troops remained significant. Across Africa, veterans, workers, religious movements and educated elites demanded different degrees of reform, protection and self-government. \[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-3" class="cite">3</a>\]\[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-15" class="cite">15</a>\]

The mandates system placed former German and Ottoman possessions under outside administration in the language of international stewardship. Supporters claimed it imposed accountability; many critics argued that it preserved empire under a more acceptable legal name. Not every independence movement sought exactly the same political future. Some demanded constitutional inclusion, others full sovereignty, social revolution, or regional federation. Their histories matter because the legitimacy of the supposedly new international order was tested in colonial streets and villages as well as European diplomatic salons. \[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-3" class="cite">3</a>\]\[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-5" class="cite">5</a>\]
