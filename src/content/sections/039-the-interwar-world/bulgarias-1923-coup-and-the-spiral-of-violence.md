On 9 June 1923 a military-backed coup overthrew Stamboliyski's government. He was captured and murdered shortly afterward. Aleksandar Tsankov headed the new government, supported by a coalition of forces opposed to the agrarian regime. The coup did not produce immediate political peace. In September 1923 a communist-led uprising was suppressed with considerable violence. The events reflected deep polarization, including conflicts among agrarians, communists, nationalists, officers, and the monarchy. \[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-33" class="cite">33</a>\]\[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-35" class="cite">35</a>\]

The spiral continued. On 16 April 1925 a bomb exploded in Sofia's St Nedelya Church during a funeral, killing and injuring many people. The attack was organized by militants associated with the communist underground; the subsequent repression included extrajudicial killings and severe restrictions on civil freedoms. It would be false to treat terrorist violence and state repression as identical acts, or to explain either without distinguishing perpetrators and victims. The cumulative consequence was a political environment in which parliamentary legitimacy and public safety were repeatedly undermined. \[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-33" class="cite">33</a>\]\[<a href="/039-the-interwar-world/sources/#source-35" class="cite">35</a>\]
