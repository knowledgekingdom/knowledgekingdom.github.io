In northeastern Brazil, communities associated with Palmares resisted colonial efforts to destroy settlements founded and sustained by people escaping enslavement. Palmares was not a single, simple village: it encompassed different settlements and political arrangements over time. Figures such as Ganga Zumba and Zumbi became important in both the historical record and later Brazilian memory. Military campaigns against Palmares show that slaveholding states devoted extensive resources to preventing independent Black communities from surviving.

Historical sources about Palmares largely originated from colonial opponents; they require careful examination. The community also faced its own political choices and conflicts, which should not be flattened into a modern utopian image. Its significance rests on the documented attempt to maintain autonomy against a slaveholding order and on the generations of people who made that autonomy possible.

Thinking geographically, remote forests and difficult approaches could offer some protection, but no refuge was completely isolated. Food supplies, trade, political alliances and information mattered. As in other chapters, landscapes shaped the options available without determining outcomes. Palmares makes resistance a history of institutions and logistics, not just heroic individuals.
