What made the Haitian Revolution possible? One answer emphasizes the outbreak of the French Revolution and its language of rights. Another stresses demographic realities in Saint-Domingue, where enslaved people formed a large majority. A third emphasizes organization, military leadership, international rivalries and strategic choices made by Black revolutionaries. These explanations need not cancel one another. Different causes operated at different levels.

A careful causal analysis distinguishes background conditions, triggers, mobilization and outcomes. The plantation system created extreme grievances and coercion; political crisis unsettled colonial authority; enslaved participants organized resistance; foreign intervention changed the military balance. Each factor interacts with others. The outcome was not predetermined by demographics or by slogans written in France.

The revolution changed the international debate about freedom, but subsequent violence and diplomatic pressure constrained Haiti's early state-building. The lesson is to resist both a heroic single-person explanation and a fatalistic idea that freedom followed automatically from the age of revolutions.
