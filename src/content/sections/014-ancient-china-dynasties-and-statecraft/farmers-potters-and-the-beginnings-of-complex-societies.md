By the Neolithic period, communities in several East Asian regions cultivated crops, raised animals, constructed settlements and created distinctive material cultures. Northern farming traditions are closely associated with millets, while the middle and lower Yangtze regions are important to the history of rice cultivation. The two stories overlapped through exchange and migration but cannot be reduced to a single origin. Later crops, including wheat from western Eurasia, entered Chinese agricultural systems through longer-distance connections. \[<a href="/014-ancient-china-dynasties-and-statecraft/sources/#source-4" class="cite">4</a>\]\[<a href="/014-ancient-china-dynasties-and-statecraft/sources/#source-5" class="cite">5</a>\]

Archaeologists use names such as Yangshao, Longshan, Hongshan and Liangzhu to discuss patterns of pottery, burials, settlement and craftsmanship. These names are classifications built from excavated evidence; they are not necessarily the names ancient people used for themselves. Painted ceramics in some Yangshao contexts, distinctive black pottery in some Longshan contexts and elaborate jade traditions in Hongshan and Liangzhu communities indicate technological variety and changing social practices. A named archaeological culture can include substantial local diversity. \[<a href="/014-ancient-china-dynasties-and-statecraft/sources/#source-4" class="cite">4</a>\]\[<a href="/014-ancient-china-dynasties-and-statecraft/sources/#source-6" class="cite">6</a>\]

A region with many productive villages may develop markets, ceremonial centers, defended settlements or elite burial grounds without instantly creating a dynasty. The evidence for social difference often grows from the comparison of ordinary and exceptional graves: who has access to jade or elaborate tomb construction, who is buried with little, and how resources were mobilized. The strongest interpretations look for patterns across many sites and generations rather than attach a king's name to a remarkable artifact. One of the most instructive sites is Liangzhu, which flourished long before the best-documented Shang rulers.
