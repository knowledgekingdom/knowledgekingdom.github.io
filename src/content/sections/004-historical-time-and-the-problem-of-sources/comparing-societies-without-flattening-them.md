Comparison helps identify patterns, but it can also create false equivalence. Imagine comparing how two states responded to a famine. You might examine weather, crop yields, trade routes, government relief measures, taxation, and the distribution of political power. But if the regions had different populations, transport networks, or record-keeping, a crude comparison of reported deaths might tell you little. An informed comparison first establishes comparable questions, units, and evidence quality.

Historians sometimes use a “most similar” approach: compare cases sharing many conditions but with different outcomes, then investigate what differed. A “most different” comparison looks for processes that recur across otherwise contrasting societies. These are guides, not mechanical proofs. We cannot usually randomize empires into controlled experiments. Identical-looking outcomes may have distinct causes. A successful revolt might arise from military collapse in one region and a negotiated constitutional transition in another.

Comparative history is strongest when it respects local context. “Why did one region industrialize sooner?” cannot responsibly be answered by claiming one population was innately more capable. Such assertions ignore technologies, institutions, colonial relationships, capital, energy supplies, disease environments, transport, and the consequences of coercion and slavery. Historical comparisons should identify what evidence would distinguish explanations—and take seriously actors who were once excluded from standard narratives.

We must also distinguish <strong>explanation</strong> from <strong>justification</strong>. Understanding why a government persecuted a minority is not approval of the persecution. Analyzing the pressures on an imperial state does not absolve crimes committed under its authority. Conversely, moral condemnation alone does not explain mechanisms, participants, failures of opposition, or the social conditions that enabled wrongdoing. Serious historical understanding can combine moral clarity with causal precision.
