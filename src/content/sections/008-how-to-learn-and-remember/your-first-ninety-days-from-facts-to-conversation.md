In the first month, focus on <strong>foundational literacy</strong>. Learn the language of maps, sources, arguments, statistics, historical periods, and institutions. Your target is to explain a term and give one accurate example. The second month introduces <strong>connections</strong>: geography and trade, political systems and economic incentives, historical sources and competing interpretations, science and uncertainty. Your target is a clear two-minute explanation linking several concepts.

In the third month, emphasize <strong>application</strong>. Read a neutral overview of a historical event and identify long-term causes and triggers. Examine a published chart and criticize or defend its presentation. Compare two explanations of a political decision. Describe a scientific mechanism and a limitation of your knowledge. Practice taking part in a conversation by asking one good question before offering a conclusion.

Progress will be uneven. Some subjects will become familiar quickly; others require repeated attempts. The best measure of improvement is whether you can answer questions that previously left you silent, recognize when an argument lacks evidence, and revise your view when you learn something new. That is not just the accumulation of trivia. It is the construction of a more capable mind.
