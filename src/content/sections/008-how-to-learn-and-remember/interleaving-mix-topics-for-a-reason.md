<strong>Interleaving</strong> means mixing related topics or problem types during practice rather than studying one type repeatedly in a single uninterrupted block. In some settings this helps learners distinguish when different concepts apply. A beginner in geography might alternate between climate diagrams, political boundaries, and transport maps, then choose which one is suitable for a particular question.

But interleaving is not a rule to change subjects every minute. When material is completely new or cognitively demanding, a focused block with several clear examples may be better for initial understanding. Later, mixing topics can help you discriminate between them. Researchers have found benefits in some domains and tasks, while the best schedule varies with content and learning stage.

For Knowledge Kingdom, I recommend <strong>one primary topic each session</strong> and a brief retrieval review from two older subjects. This preserves narrative immersion while making the knowledge more connected. For example, on the day you study Mesopotamia, review a question on river geography and one on how historians evaluate primary sources. Three subjects appear, but one remains the center of attention.
