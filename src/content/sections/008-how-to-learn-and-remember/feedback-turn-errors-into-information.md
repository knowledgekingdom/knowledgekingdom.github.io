A learning system needs feedback, as Chapter 7 explained. If you answer a quiz incorrectly, identify the type of error. Did you misremember a fact? Confuse two similar concepts? Apply the right formula to the wrong denominator? Overstate a historical cause? Give an opinion without evidence? The correction should target the reason, not just replace one answer with another.

A simple <strong>error log</strong> might record: 'Confused 1878 autonomy with 1908 independence. Correction: different legal statuses. Revisit next week.' That note is more valuable than a generic mark of failure. It tells your future self exactly what needs to be strengthened. Over time, patterns may emerge: perhaps you need more map practice, clearer definitions, or better attention to qualifiers such as 'sometimes' and 'always.'

An informal weekly checkpoint can have four parts: five factual questions, two short explanations, one application problem, and a brief reflection on what remains unclear. Record improvement in understanding, not just pages consumed. If you consistently cannot explain a concept, slow down and rebuild its foundations.
