A good note is short enough to revisit and meaningful enough to reconstruct an idea. You might use three columns: <strong>Question</strong>, <strong>Answer in my own words</strong>, and <strong>Connection or example</strong>. Instead of copying six paragraphs about inflation, write: 'What is inflation? A sustained rise in the general price level, not every price rising equally. Example: purchasing power declines if wages do not keep pace.'

Separate facts from your own interpretations. Use dates for historical claims. For scientific claims, note whether the statement is well established or a working explanation. If a sentence seems surprising, preserve the source. Your notes should make it possible to correct your understanding rather than freezing the first explanation you encountered.

Flashcards suit brief, discriminable questions: 'What does sovereign mean?' or 'What is the difference between a mean and a median?' They are less suitable as the only tool for complicated essay questions. A hundred isolated cards cannot substitute for a reasoned explanation of why an empire declined. Pair short recall with open-ended writing, maps, calculations, and discussion.
