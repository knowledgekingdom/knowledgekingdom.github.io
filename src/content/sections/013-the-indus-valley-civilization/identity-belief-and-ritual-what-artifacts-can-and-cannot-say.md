There are terracotta figurines, animal images, decorated pottery, ornaments, architecture and objects that invite religious interpretations. Some scholars have linked particular figures to mother-goddess traditions or later South Asian deities; others caution that this may project much later religious systems backward onto material we cannot read. An image of a person seated among animals, for example, can be described accurately without securely identifying the person, a god or the precise ritual. \[<a href="/013-the-indus-valley-civilization/sources/#source-4" class="cite">4</a>\]\[<a href="/013-the-indus-valley-civilization/sources/#source-15" class="cite">15</a>\]

The Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro has often been described as ritual, and that remains a plausible interpretation because of its distinctive construction and setting. Yet we cannot name its prayers or establish that all communities used water in the same way. Domestic bathing might also have practical, social and symbolic dimensions. Modern categories—religious, hygienic, political—do not always divide ancient activities cleanly. They are useful analytical tools, not proven labels for every ancient room.

Burial customs vary across sites and periods. Grave goods and human remains help researchers ask about identity, family, beliefs and inequality, but there is no surviving authoritative Harappan book of the dead. Objects accompanying the dead are evidence of practices and possible meanings, not a complete map of an afterlife doctrine. This is why it would be misleading to claim to know exactly what an Indus person believed about death.

The larger point is respectful caution. Religious heritage can matter deeply to living communities, and the temptation to find a cherished tradition in a distant past is understandable. However, historical continuity needs demonstration, not only resemblance. Some influences may indeed be long-lasting, but specific continuity claims should be compared with changes, gaps in evidence and alternative interpretations. A society should not have to be made into somebody else's ancestor to deserve historical attention.
