<strong>Before c. 3300 BCE:</strong> Earlier farming, herding and settlement traditions develop across different parts of the wider region, including communities such as Mehrgarh.

<strong>c. 3300–2600 BCE:</strong> Broad Early Harappan phase; settlements, crafts and exchange networks develop differently across regions.

<strong>c. 2600–1900 BCE:</strong> Mature Harappan urban phase, including major centers at Harappa, Mohenjo-daro and Dholavira.

<strong>Third millennium BCE:</strong> Indus materials and merchants participate in extended networks reaching the Gulf and Mesopotamia; exact chronology varies by site.

<strong>After c. 1900 BCE:</strong> Widespread urban networks reorganize; many large settlements contract, while regional communities continue and change.

<strong>c. 1900–1300 BCE and later:</strong> Broad Late Harappan traditions and subsequent regional histories; the transition was neither uniform nor a complete population disappearance.

<strong>1920s CE:</strong> The significance of Harappa and Mohenjo-daro as remains of an ancient urban civilization becomes internationally recognized through archaeological research.

These are approximate teaching ranges. Ancient chronologies and local phase names differ, and not every site follows the same transitions.
