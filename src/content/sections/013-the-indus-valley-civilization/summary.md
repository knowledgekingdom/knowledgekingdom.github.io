<strong>Core argument.</strong> The Indus civilization was an extensive, diverse Bronze Age urban tradition in present-day Pakistan and northwestern India. It created cities with sophisticated water and street infrastructure and participated in shared systems of bricks, weights, seals, crafts and long-distance exchange. Because its signs are not securely deciphered and political evidence is incomplete, many claims about rulers, religion and language remain uncertain. Environmental changes and shifting networks contributed to a regionally varied transformation after around 1900 BCE, rather than an instant disappearance of its people.
