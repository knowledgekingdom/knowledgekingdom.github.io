Voltaire used essays, historical writing, fiction, correspondence, and public campaigns to criticize intolerance and abuses of power. The Calas affair in France, involving the wrongful execution of Jean Calas in 1762, became a symbol of the struggle against religious prejudice and judicial injustice. Voltaire's writings combined sharp literary skill with engagement in practical controversies.

Yet Voltaire was no simple modern democrat, and his opinions included prejudices and contradictions. His ideal of enlightened reform often relied on educated elites and rulers. Defending toleration in one context does not guarantee sympathy for all groups in another.

What makes the case historically important is the developing relationship between printed opinion and official authority. An injustice could become the subject of a transregional campaign, affecting the reputation of judges and governments. Public criticism was dangerous and often restricted, but it began to acquire an institutional and political force of its own.
