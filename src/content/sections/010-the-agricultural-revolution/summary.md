<strong>Core argument.</strong> The Agricultural Revolution was a collection of long, uneven transitions from reliance on wild resources toward varying forms of cultivation, domestication, herding and settlement. It changed human relationships with food, land and one another, and it was neither one invention nor a guaranteed improvement in every dimension of life.

<strong>Essential terms.</strong> Foraging; sedentism; cultivation; domestication; agriculture; niche construction; Fertile Crescent; Natufian; rachis; nonshattering trait; phytolith; vegetative propagation; tell; granary; surplus; subsistence; pastoralism; irrigation; food security; trade-off; archaeological inference; demic diffusion; cultural diffusion.

To Chapter 7: feedback, stocks and delays in crop production and population. To Chapter 11: food production created opportunities for political organization, but it did not automatically make the first states.
