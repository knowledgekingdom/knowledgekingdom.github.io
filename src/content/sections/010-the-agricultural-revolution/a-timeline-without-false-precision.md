Instead of memorizing one mythical invention date, retain several approximate anchors and the kinds of evidence they represent. The terminal Pleistocene and early Holocene, roughly the period around 12,000–10,000 BCE and after, saw important changes in several human food economies, but practices in some places began earlier and fully agricultural livelihoods often appeared later. Southwest Asian societies show early cultivation and management of crops and animals through a prolonged transition. East Asian crops followed their own long histories of use, cultivation and domestication. In New Guinea, evidence of wetland cultivation is particularly important from roughly 7,000–6,400 years ago. \[<a href="/010-the-agricultural-revolution/sources/#source-1" class="cite">1</a>\]\[<a href="/010-the-agricultural-revolution/sources/#source-7" class="cite">7</a>\]\[<a href="/010-the-agricultural-revolution/sources/#source-8" class="cite">8</a>\]

At Çatalhöyük, the east mound's principal Neolithic occupation is dated about 7400–6200 BCE. Agriculture spread across the Aegean and southeastern Europe in the seventh millennium BCE, with later expansion across other European regions. The Varna cemetery belongs thousands of years after those first farmers, in the fifth millennium BCE. The dates are memorable only if you remember the distinctions between <em>origin</em>, <em>spread</em>, <em>settlement growth</em>, and <em>later social transformation</em>. \[<a href="/010-the-agricultural-revolution/sources/#source-11" class="cite">11</a>\]\[<a href="/010-the-agricultural-revolution/sources/#source-13" class="cite">13</a>\]\[<a href="/010-the-agricultural-revolution/sources/#source-14" class="cite">14</a>\]

The most important chronological insight is scale. Our species has existed for hundreds of thousands of years; agriculture became prominent only in a comparatively recent portion of that history. Once present, it did not follow one synchronized calendar. A farming village in Southwest Asia, a wetland garden in New Guinea, and a developing maize system in the Americas cannot be plotted as steps on a single evolutionary staircase.
