<strong>Myth 1: One person invented farming.</strong> Reality: people in multiple regions gradually developed cultivation, management and domestication through distinct histories. Some practices spread; others arose locally.

<strong>Myth 2: Farmers were always healthier and worked less.</strong> Reality: labor, diet, disease and security changed in many ways, with results differing by crop, ecology and social arrangements.

<strong>Myth 3: A permanent village proves agriculture.</strong> Reality: some relatively settled groups used wild resources extensively; settlement and agriculture need separate evidence.

<strong>Myth 4: Agriculture immediately created inequality and kings.</strong> Reality: long intervals and different institutions intervened. Some large differences in material wealth emerged much later, and farming did not produce a single universal political structure.

<strong>Myth 5: The Fertile Crescent invented all farming for the whole world.</strong> Reality: Southwest Asia was enormously important, but East Asia, the Americas, Africa and New Guinea also contain histories of local domestication and agricultural development.
