A timeline helps prevent distinct events from collapsing into one 'age of nationalism.' The French Revolution began in 1789 and helped popularize new claims about national sovereignty. Vienna in 1814–1815 restored a dynastic diplomatic order while leaving national grievances. Greek independence developed after 1821 and received international recognition in the early 1830s. Revolutions swept much of Europe in 1848–1849. Italy proclaimed a kingdom in 1861 and added major territories in 1866 and 1870. Prussian wars reshaped Germany between 1864 and 1871, culminating in the empire's proclamation in January 1871. \[<a href="/036-nationalism-and-the-nineteenth-century-nation-state/sources/#source-8" class="cite">8</a>\]\[<a href="/036-nationalism-and-the-nineteenth-century-nation-state/sources/#source-13" class="cite">13</a>\]\[<a href="/036-nationalism-and-the-nineteenth-century-nation-state/sources/#source-15" class="cite">15</a>\]

Parallel timelines matter. Russia suppressed the January Uprising in 1863–1864. Austria–Hungary was established in 1867. Ottoman reform had been developing since 1839, and an Ottoman constitution was promulgated in 1876. Bulgarian church autonomy was recognized in 1870, the April Uprising occurred in 1876, and the 1878 Berlin settlement created a new autonomous Bulgarian principality. Japan's Meiji transformations followed 1868. \[<a href="/036-nationalism-and-the-nineteenth-century-nation-state/sources/#source-17" class="cite">17</a>\]\[<a href="/036-nationalism-and-the-nineteenth-century-nation-state/sources/#source-21" class="cite">21</a>\]\[<a href="/036-nationalism-and-the-nineteenth-century-nation-state/sources/#source-25" class="cite">25</a>\]

The lesson is not to memorize dates as though they caused events. Each date marks one stage in a longer process. National schools could precede sovereignty; sovereignty could precede mass democracy; borders could be revised after national states existed. A useful chronology asks what changed institutionally before and after each milestone.
