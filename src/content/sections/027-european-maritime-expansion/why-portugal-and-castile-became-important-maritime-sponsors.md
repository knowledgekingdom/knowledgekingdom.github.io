Portugal's Atlantic-facing ports, established seafaring communities, royal institutions and commercial interest in African gold helped sustain voyages along western Africa. The Castilian and Aragonese crowns followed different political and maritime trajectories, and their eventual joint monarchy could support westward expeditions. Dynastic alliances, state finance and rivalries mattered, but neither kingdom had unlimited resources. Overseas ventures depended on partnerships with merchants, skilled navigators, private investors, foreign specialists and local allies. \[<a href="/027-european-maritime-expansion/sources/#source-1" class="cite">1</a>\]\[<a href="/027-european-maritime-expansion/sources/#source-6" class="cite">6</a>\]\[<a href="/027-european-maritime-expansion/sources/#source-7" class="cite">7</a>\]

Iberian expansion also had a religious dimension. Centuries of warfare, coexistence, diplomacy and commerce among Christian, Muslim and Jewish communities on the peninsula shaped the political vocabulary of rulers. The 1492 conquest of Granada and pressure toward religious uniformity formed part of the wider context of Castilian policy. Crusading ideals did not explain every voyage, but missions to spread Christianity, find potential Christian allies and challenge Muslim powers appear repeatedly in sponsors' stated aims. Commercial and religious purposes could reinforce or contradict one another. \[<a href="/027-european-maritime-expansion/sources/#source-1" class="cite">1</a>\]\[<a href="/027-european-maritime-expansion/sources/#source-3" class="cite">3</a>\]\[<a href="/027-european-maritime-expansion/sources/#source-7" class="cite">7</a>\]

It would be inaccurate to picture Spain and Portugal as unified modern nation-states with settled public opinion supporting expansion. Different ports and court factions competed for privileges; investors negotiated for returns; and captains sometimes acted against crown orders. Changes at sea therefore reflect not a single 'European will' but the interaction of royal power, private enterprise, military institutions and existing maritime skills.
