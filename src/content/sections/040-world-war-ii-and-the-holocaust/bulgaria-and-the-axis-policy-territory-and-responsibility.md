Bulgaria's path through the war was not identical to that of Germany or Italy. It joined the Axis in March 1941, participated in occupation and administration in parts of Yugoslavia and Greece, and declared war on Britain and the United States in December 1941. It did not join Germany's 1941 invasion of the Soviet Union, maintaining diplomatic relations with Moscow until 1944. Bulgarian authorities introduced antisemitic legislation, including the Law for the Protection of the Nation, restricting Jewish civil and economic life. State policy was therefore discriminatory even before the deportation plans of 1943.

The country's government sought territorial revision after the settlement of 1919 and operated under strong German pressure, but diplomatic constraints do not erase the agency of Bulgarian officials. Nor should the military occupation of Vardar Macedonia, parts of Greek Thrace and Pirot be described as simply reunification accepted by all inhabitants. Some local people welcomed the administration, others resisted, and occupation exposed communities to new legal classifications and coercion.
