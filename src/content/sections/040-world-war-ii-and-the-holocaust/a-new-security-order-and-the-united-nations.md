Allied leaders met during the war to coordinate military strategy and discuss postwar arrangements. Tehran, Yalta and Potsdam shaped decisions about occupation, borders and the future of Germany, but no conference alone determined the fate of every country. Allied military occupations created new political realities, while differences between the Soviet Union and Western powers deepened. Germany was divided into occupation zones; Japan came under U.S.-led occupation. Postwar reconstruction required choices about food, industry, political responsibility and legitimate government.

The United Nations Charter was signed at San Francisco on 26 June 1945 and entered into force on 24 October. The new organization combined a General Assembly with a Security Council giving special authority to five permanent members. Its structure reflected both the wish for international cooperation and the power inequalities of 1945. The war also accelerated development of international human rights law. Such institutions did not abolish war, but they changed the vocabulary and some mechanisms through which states and citizens would contest the use of force and the treatment of people. \[<a href="/040-world-war-ii-and-the-holocaust/sources/#source-33" class="cite">33</a>\]\[<a href="/040-world-war-ii-and-the-holocaust/sources/#source-36" class="cite">36</a>\]
