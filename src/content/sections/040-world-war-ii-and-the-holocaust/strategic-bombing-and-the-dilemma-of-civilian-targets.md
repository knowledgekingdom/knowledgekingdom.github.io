The Allied bombing campaign against Germany and Japan targeted factories, transport, fuel systems and urban areas, sometimes deliberately attacking entire built-up districts. Germany and Japan had earlier bombed civilian cities, including Warsaw, London, Chongqing and others. Air power could disrupt production but also killed civilians and destroyed housing, hospitals and cultural heritage. Technical accuracy was limited, especially at night. Historians debate the extent to which area bombing hastened victory, diverted resources, undermined morale or crossed moral and legal lines.

An evaluation must separate a bomber crew's operational orders from political decisions about target selection and the broader laws and ethics of war. Evidence includes raid records, industrial output, civilian death estimates, aircraft losses and the alternatives available to decision-makers at the time. It is possible to recognize Nazi Germany's aggression and genocidal crimes while still asking whether particular Allied bombing methods were justified. Judging the conduct of warfare requires consistent standards, attention to context and refusal to romanticize suffering. \[<a href="/040-world-war-ii-and-the-holocaust/sources/#source-7" class="cite">7</a>\]\[<a href="/040-world-war-ii-and-the-holocaust/sources/#source-31" class="cite">31</a>\]
