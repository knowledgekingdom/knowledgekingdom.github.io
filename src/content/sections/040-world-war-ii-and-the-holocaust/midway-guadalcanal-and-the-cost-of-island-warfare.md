The naval battle of Midway in June 1942 cost Japan four fleet carriers and reduced its ability to conduct offensive carrier operations. American intelligence, tactical decisions, damage control and chance all contributed. Midway is often called a turning point, but it did not instantly end Japanese offensives or make the rest of the war easy. The extended Guadalcanal campaign from August 1942 to early 1943 involved attrition on land, at sea and in the air. It required fuel, supply, replacement personnel and command endurance more than one decisive maneuver.

Pacific combat often took place on islands whose populations suffered displacement, disease and the destruction of food supplies. Later campaigns on Saipan, Iwo Jima and Okinawa revealed the devastating consequences of fighting against fortified positions and political expectations of resistance to the end. American forces also drew on Australian, New Zealand and other Allied support, while Chinese armies continued to pin down large Japanese forces on the Asian mainland.
