Following the German attack on the Soviet Union, SS and police units, including the Einsatzgruppen, together with German forces and collaborators, murdered Jews in mass shootings across occupied eastern Europe. At Babi Yar near Kyiv, tens of thousands of Jewish people were shot over two days in September 1941. Similar operations struck communities in the Baltic region, Belarus, Ukraine and elsewhere. A large part of Holocaust killing happened outside gas chambers; any account that equates the Holocaust exclusively with Auschwitz omits victims and methods.

Many killings involved local knowledge, auxiliary police, seized property and coercive occupation systems. Participation and resistance varied among occupied communities; responsibility cannot be assigned indiscriminately to an entire people. Victims were often murdered near places where they had lived, before any transport to a camp was possible. Recovering local names, records and burial sites is an ethical and scholarly task because anonymous totals alone cannot convey the destruction of distinct families and communities. Source criticism requires combining perpetrator reports, survivor testimony, photographs, forensic findings and demographic comparisons. \[<a href="/040-world-war-ii-and-the-holocaust/sources/#source-19" class="cite">19</a>\]\[<a href="/040-world-war-ii-and-the-holocaust/sources/#source-22" class="cite">22</a>\]
