The Mediterranean theater involved control of sea lanes, ports, the Suez Canal and access to Middle Eastern resources. Italian attacks on British-held positions in Egypt and failures in the Balkans prompted German interventions. The fighting in Libya, Egypt and Tunisia pitted mobile units against desert conditions that made supply particularly difficult. Rommel's reputation, reinforced by later popular histories, should not eclipse Allied command decisions, the importance of fuel and transport, or the experiences of local people. El Alamein in 1942 and the Anglo-American landings in northwest Africa helped reverse Axis momentum.

North African warfare relied on troops from Britain, France, Australia, New Zealand, India and other societies. Colonized people were not just scenery. Many fought, carried supplies or faced requisitioning and bombing in territories they had not chosen to turn into battlefields. The Allied victory in North Africa in 1943 opened the way to invasion of Sicily and then mainland Italy.
