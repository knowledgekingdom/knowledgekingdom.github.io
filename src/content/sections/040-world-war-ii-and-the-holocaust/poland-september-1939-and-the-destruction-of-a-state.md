Germany attacked Poland on 1 September 1939 using combined arms, concentrated firepower, mobile formations and air operations. The often-used word <em>Blitzkrieg</em> can conceal the diversity of German tactics and the fierce Polish resistance. On 3 September Britain and France declared war on Germany. On 17 September the Soviet Union invaded eastern Poland under arrangements connected to the secret protocols of the Nazi-Soviet pact of August 1939. Poland was divided between two occupying powers whose systems differed radically but whose policies both imposed coercion, displacement and repression.

The destruction of Polish sovereignty was also an attack on its society. Nazi authorities pursued killings of political and intellectual elites, seized property and began measures directed against Jewish communities; Soviet authorities carried out deportations and executions, including the Katyn massacre of Polish officers and others in 1940. A state can lose formal control long before its inhabitants cease to resist. Polish government institutions reconstituted themselves abroad, underground networks organized schools and intelligence, and soldiers continued fighting on Allied fronts.
