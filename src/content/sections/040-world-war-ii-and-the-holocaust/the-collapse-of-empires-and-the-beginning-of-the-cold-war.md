European colonial powers survived the war in name, but their economic resources and claims to political legitimacy were weakened. Japan's defeats ended its colonial empire and transformed political opportunities across Asia. Independence movements in Indonesia, Vietnam, India and elsewhere drew on wartime experience, local organizing and international change, although their paths and chronologies were distinct. Britain and France attempted to restore or manage imperial rule, often meeting powerful resistance. The story of decolonization requires its own chapter, but its origins are inseparable from the war.

The alliance between the Soviet Union and Western democracies was built on the need to defeat the Axis, not agreement about postwar political systems. Competing security goals, occupation arrangements, ideology and mistrust produced the early Cold War. In eastern Europe, the Soviet presence enabled Communist governments to consolidate; in Western Europe and Japan, U.S. economic and strategic influence expanded. The same conflict that ended fascist imperial regimes thus helped establish a world increasingly organized around rival superpowers.
