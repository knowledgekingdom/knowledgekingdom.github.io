World War II was humanity's deadliest war, but exact global losses cannot be counted with the precision of modern census records. Common estimates run into many tens of millions of deaths, often roughly 60 to 80 million or more depending on included causes, territories and methods. Combat fatalities are only part of the total. Hunger, mass executions, genocide, forced labor, siege, disease and displacement killed civilians at a staggering rate. Many people died where records were destroyed or never produced, especially in parts of Asia and eastern Europe.

The appropriate historical practice is to identify the population and method underlying any number. A figure for 'the Soviet Union' may combine military and civilian deaths; a figure for 'China' may vary according to whether it begins in 1937 or includes earlier campaigns. Different statistical methods estimate excess mortality, registered deaths or documented victims. The Holocaust's approximately six million Jewish victims are independently established through many forms of evidence, but some local counts and individual identities remain under investigation.
