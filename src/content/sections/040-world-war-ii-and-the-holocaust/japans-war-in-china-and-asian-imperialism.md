The Japanese army had occupied Manchuria in 1931 and a full-scale Sino-Japanese war developed in 1937. Japanese forces seized major cities but faced Chinese Nationalist armies, Communist forces, local armed groups and continued civilian resistance. The fall of Nanjing in December 1937 was followed by a massacre, mass sexual violence and other atrocities by Japanese troops; the precise victim total is contested, but the crimes are well documented. War in China preceded the European conflict and would continue until 1945, profoundly shaping the wider Asian struggle.

The Japanese empire advanced a vision of regional liberation from Western colonial power while imposing its own coercive occupation and economic extraction. Some Asian nationalist leaders made tactical arrangements with Japan in hopes of independence; others resisted, and many local societies were divided. Policies toward prisoners of war and civilians included forced labor, starvation, mass killings and sexual slavery, including the system of so-called 'comfort women.' Victims and perpetrator institutions must remain visible behind military labels.
