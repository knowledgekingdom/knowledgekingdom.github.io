Nazi Germany persecuted and murdered many non-Jewish people, including Roma and Sinti, people with disabilities, Soviet prisoners of war, Polish civilians and elites, gay men, Jehovah's Witnesses, political opponents and others. These victim groups were subjected to differing policies, not one identical program. The murder of people with disabilities through the so-called 'euthanasia' program involved medical institutions and personnel. The genocide of Roma and Sinti destroyed communities through mass shootings, deportations, forced labor and killings in camps. Millions of Soviet POWs died in German custody through starvation, exposure, disease and murder.

Recognizing all these crimes does not require collapsing their histories into a single category. The Holocaust refers specifically to the genocide of European Jews, while the Nazis committed additional genocidal and mass-atrocity crimes. Distinctions matter because they preserve historical mechanisms and targeted identities. It is equally wrong to use other victims to diminish the murder of Jews or to discuss Jewish suffering as if no other groups were persecuted. The challenge is to describe each system accurately and examine how racist ideology and state institutions made violence possible. \[<a href="/040-world-war-ii-and-the-holocaust/sources/#source-19" class="cite">19</a>\]\[<a href="/040-world-war-ii-and-the-holocaust/sources/#source-24" class="cite">24</a>\]
