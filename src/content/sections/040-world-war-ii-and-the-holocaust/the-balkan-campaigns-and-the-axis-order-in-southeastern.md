Germany intervened in Yugoslavia and Greece in April 1941 after political and military crises in the region. Yugoslavia's rapid defeat was followed by partition, occupation and competing local regimes, including the genocidal Ustaša state in Croatia. Resistance developed within complex civil conflicts involving Partisans, Chetniks and various collaborators; these movements cannot be reduced to one unified national story. Greece experienced occupation, famine, reprisals and resistance. The war intensified preexisting disputes over territory and identity and caused new cycles of civilian suffering.

Bulgaria joined the Tripartite Pact on 1 March 1941. Bulgarian forces participated in occupation administration in territories taken from Yugoslavia and Greece, including Vardar Macedonia and parts of Thrace, without those territories becoming internationally recognized sovereign Bulgarian lands. The distinction between occupied and prewar territory would have fatal consequences for Jewish communities in 1943. Bulgaria also regained Southern Dobruja from Romania in 1940 through the Treaty of Craiova.
