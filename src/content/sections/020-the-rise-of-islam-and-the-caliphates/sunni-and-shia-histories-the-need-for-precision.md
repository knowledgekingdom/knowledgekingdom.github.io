<strong>Sunni</strong> and <strong>Shia</strong> Islam developed into broad traditions of belief, law, ritual and communal memory, but their origins cannot be described solely as one election dispute. The designation <strong>Shi'at Ali</strong>, or party/supporters of Ali, points toward an early allegiance; the full forms of later Shia teaching concerning the <strong>imamate</strong> emerged across subsequent generations. Sunni traditions developed through their own debates about leadership, legal authority, religious knowledge and the early caliphs. Neither tradition is internally uniform. The Twelver Shia, Ismaili Shia and Zaydi Shia follow distinct histories; Sunni legal and theological schools likewise exhibit important diversity.

The <strong>Battle of Karbala (680)</strong> became central to Shia historical and devotional memory. Husayn ibn Ali, Muhammad's grandson, and a small group of companions and relatives were killed by forces associated with the Umayyad regime under <strong>Yazid I</strong>. For many Shia Muslims, Husayn's stand embodies faithfulness and opposition to injustice; his martyrdom is mourned especially during <strong>Ashura</strong>. Sunni Muslims also hold Husayn in esteem, though interpretations and commemorative practices vary. This chapter will not turn sacred grief into spectacle or present every contested detail as though it were independently documented.

A historian should ask three different questions: <strong>What happened? How did contemporaries and later communities remember it? What theological conclusions did they draw?</strong> Those questions overlap but are not interchangeable. Some beliefs cannot be adjudicated by examining an army roster or an inscription; some political claims can be checked against multiple written and material sources. The history of Sunni–Shia difference is therefore simultaneously a story of political conflict, religious reasoning, and memory. It is not a justification for treating present-day Muslims as bound to repeat the antagonisms of the seventh century.
