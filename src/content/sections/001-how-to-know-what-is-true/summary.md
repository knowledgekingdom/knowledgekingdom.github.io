1\. Truth and confidence are different: reality does not depend on our certainty.

2\. Separate descriptive facts, causal explanations, predictions, and value judgments.

3\. Observation depends on instruments, categories, attention, and interpretation.

4\. Evidence must fit the specific question being asked.

5\. Trace information to its origin and seek genuinely independent confirmation.

6\. A correlation can be useful without proving a particular causal explanation.

7\. Science grows through transparent methods, criticism, replication, and revision.

8\. Relevant expertise matters, but no individual's status makes a claim infallible.

9\. Update beliefs in proportion to new evidence, not the intensity of a reaction.

10\. Good skepticism can sometimes lead to agreement; reflexive opposition cannot.
