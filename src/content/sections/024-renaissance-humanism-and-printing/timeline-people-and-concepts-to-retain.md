A concise timeline helps us keep sequences distinct. Petrarch's lifetime (1304–1374) belongs to the emergence of Italian humanism; the early fifteenth century saw growing Greek study and artistic experiment; Brunelleschi's dome was built from 1420 to 1436; Gutenberg's printing system matured in the mid-1450s; the Cetinje Octoechos appeared in 1494; and Erasmus's Greek New Testament was printed in 1516. These dates do not mark a simple transfer of leadership from one genius to another. Different institutions and technologies overlapped across many locations.

The concepts are just as important as the chronology. Humanism, in this historical setting, refers primarily to a program of study associated with classical language, literature, history, rhetoric and moral thought. Philology tests texts through language, comparison and historical context. Patronage connects cultural production to sponsors and institutions. Linear perspective is one structured way of depicting spatial recession. Movable type enables the rearrangement of reusable letters into repeated printed pages. Incunabula are books printed during Europe's early typographic period, conventionally before 1501.

Do not confuse names with explanations. Saying 'Gutenberg invented modernity' teaches almost nothing; explaining reusable type, fixed preparation costs, business risk and distribution gives a causal mechanism. Saying 'Florence created the Renaissance' ignores the city's dependence on wealth, craft and transregional learning; tracing the links shows why the city mattered. A useful final exercise is to explain one artistic achievement and one printing innovation without relying on the word genius. If you can do that, you are moving from memorized facts to historical understanding.
