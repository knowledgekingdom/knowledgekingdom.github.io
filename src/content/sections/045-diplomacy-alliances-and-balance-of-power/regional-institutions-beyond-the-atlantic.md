ASEAN's Treaty of Amity and Cooperation in Southeast Asia, signed in 1976, sets out principles of peaceful relations, noninterference and settlement of disputes. ASEAN's institutional practice has placed substantial weight on consultation and consensus. This can make cooperation acceptable to governments that guard their autonomy, while creating difficulties where rapid or forceful collective action requires agreement among states with different interests. \[<a href="/045-diplomacy-alliances-and-balance-of-power/sources/#source-30" class="cite">30</a>\]

The African Union's Peace and Security Council was established through a protocol adopted in 2002 that entered into force in 2003. It is a regional collective-security and conflict-prevention arrangement with responsibilities that differ from those of a traditional defence alliance. Its operation depends on member governments, regional mechanisms, resources and partnerships. \[<a href="/045-diplomacy-alliances-and-balance-of-power/sources/#source-31" class="cite">31</a>\]

Comparing organisations only by asking which is most like NATO is a poor method. Different institutions emerged from different experiences of colonialism, sovereignty, regional conflict and economic integration. Their goals, authority, membership and decision rules are not interchangeable. The useful comparison asks what problem each institution was designed to address, what resources it can mobilise, whose voices are represented and where its procedures can fail.
