In 1972 US President Richard Nixon visited the People's Republic of China. The opening came after years of hostility and amid the worsening relationship between Beijing and Moscow. The Shanghai Communiqué recorded areas of agreement and differences, including the sensitive question of Taiwan. Full diplomatic relations between the United States and the PRC were established in 1979. The 1972 visit did not itself create a formal alliance or settle all disputes. \[<a href="/045-diplomacy-alliances-and-balance-of-power/sources/#source-26" class="cite">26</a>\]

The case illustrates how shifts in perceived interests can permit cooperation across deep ideological divides. It also shows how symbolic public contacts and more sustained official exchanges can work together. The well-known table-tennis exchanges were a visible component of a wider opening rather than its sole cause. To explain the change, historians must consider Chinese as well as American decisions, domestic politics and the international context.

A rapprochement is a reduction of hostility or improvement in relations. It does not imply that one side approves of the other's political system, and it does not require permanent alignment. When evaluating a diplomatic change today, ask which policy domains have changed, which disagreements persist and what alternative explanations the evidence supports. A dramatic photograph may mark an important shift without being the shift itself.
