Diplomacy is also about trade, investment, migration, energy and standards. Governments negotiate access to markets and coordinate responses to transnational risks. Economic relationships can create incentives for stability because interruption is costly. But interdependence does not guarantee peace. The distribution of dependency matters: one party may find alternative suppliers or customers more easily than another.

Economic restrictions may seek to influence behaviour or enforce legal obligations. Their effects depend on the design, international participation, alternative markets and the circumstances of affected populations. A measure directed at a government can also impose costs on civilians and third countries. Distinguish the stated objective of a sanction from empirical evidence of its effects; effectiveness and legality require separate analysis. Do not equate economic loss automatically with achievement of a political objective.

Soft power, associated with Joseph Nye, concerns influence through attraction rather than payment or compulsion. A country's cultural reputation, institutions and perceived legitimacy may make others more willing to cooperate. It is not synonymous with all nonmilitary action: an economic threat is coercion even without the use of force.
