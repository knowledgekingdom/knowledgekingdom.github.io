<strong>Core idea.</strong> A numerical claim is reliable only insofar as its definitions, measurement process, comparison, uncertainty and interpretation are defensible. The correct calculation can still answer the wrong question.

<strong>Essential terms.</strong> Population; sample; sampling frame; parameter; statistic; selection bias; nonresponse bias; weight; mean; median; mode; weighted mean; skew; outlier; standard deviation; interquartile range; denominator; incidence rate; standardized rate; index number; base year; percentage point; histogram; scatterplot; log scale; correlation; confounding; ecological fallacy; Simpson's paradox; randomized experiment; regression; confidence interval; p-value; multiple testing; missingness; provenance.

<strong>Try explaining it aloud.</strong> A news website reports, “Support for the policy rose from 40 percent to 50 percent.” Without notes, explain the difference between the ten-percentage-point increase and the 25-percent relative increase; identify three questions about the poll; describe one reason the result may not mean the policy caused the change; and say how you would check the original evidence.
