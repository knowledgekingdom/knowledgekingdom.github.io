<strong>Before c. 3100 BCE:</strong> Predynastic communities, regional centers and changing burial and exchange patterns.

<strong>Around 3100 BCE:</strong> Early dynastic unification associated in modern accounts with rulers such as Narmer and Aha.

<strong>c. 27th–22nd centuries BCE:</strong> Old Kingdom, with Djoser and the major fourth-dynasty pyramids.

<strong>Late 3rd millennium BCE:</strong> First Intermediate Period and changing regional powers.

<strong>c. 2030–1650 BCE:</strong> Middle Kingdom; reunification, court culture and stronger administration.

<strong>c. 17th–16th centuries BCE:</strong> Second Intermediate Period, including Hyksos rulers in northern Egypt.

<strong>c. 1550–1070 BCE:</strong> New Kingdom, including Hatshepsut, Akhenaten and Ramesses II.

<strong>c. 8th–7th centuries BCE:</strong> Kushite Twenty-fifth Dynasty rules Egypt.

<strong>332 BCE:</strong> Alexander takes control of Egypt.

<strong>30 BCE:</strong> Egypt passes under Roman rule following the defeat of Cleopatra VII and Mark Antony.

Ancient chronological ranges are approximate and differ among modern scholarly schemes, especially before the first millennium BCE. Dynasties were not always sequential or coterminous with uniformly ruled borders.
