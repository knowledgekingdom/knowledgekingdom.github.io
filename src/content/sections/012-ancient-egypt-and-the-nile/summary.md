<strong>Core argument.</strong> The Nile created major opportunities for agriculture and communication, but those opportunities demanded maintenance and political decisions. Ancient Egypt developed unusually durable traditions of territorial kingship, monumental building, religious practice and record-keeping. Its history nevertheless includes fragmented government, social inequalities, religious reform, imperial expansion, and deep connections with other African and western Asian societies. Surviving records privilege some people and episodes over others; comparison among sources is essential.
