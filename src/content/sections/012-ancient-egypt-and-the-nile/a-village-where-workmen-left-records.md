The settlement of Deir el-Medina, associated with artisans who constructed and decorated royal tombs near Thebes during the New Kingdom, provides an unusually rich view of work and domestic life. Ostraca—pieces of pottery or stone used for writing and drawing—and papyri preserve accounts, complaints, private messages, sketches, school exercises and other documents. A single community does not represent all Egyptians, yet it can reveal texture that monuments hide. \[<a href="/012-ancient-egypt-and-the-nile/sources/#source-19" class="cite">19</a>\]

Imagine two kinds of evidence. A temple relief shows a king presenting an offering to a god. A workmen's administrative record lists deliveries of food rations or absences from work. Both are authentic kinds of Egyptian evidence, but they answer different historical questions. The temple image informs us about ideology and ritual; the account informs us about the operation of a particular institution. Neither on its own can tell us whether a worker was happy, or whether a household had adequate food every month.

Egyptian work crews could receive distributions such as grain and other supplies rather than payment in a modern salaried-money system. When supplies were interrupted, records from the later New Kingdom document work stoppages and complaints, often presented in popular accounts as an early known labor strike. The precise events should be understood within the royal tomb workforce, whose organization and access to writing were distinctive. That distinction is important before we generalize about labor conditions across all of Egypt.

Everyday life also included child care, food preparation, textile work, household religion, relationships, education for some, and disputes over property. The living arrangements of officials and royal workers differ from those of many rural cultivators. A substantial proportion of ancient evidence reflects wealthier households or institutions that could produce durable records. Archaeology of ordinary dwellings, tools and food remains helps correct the imbalance.
