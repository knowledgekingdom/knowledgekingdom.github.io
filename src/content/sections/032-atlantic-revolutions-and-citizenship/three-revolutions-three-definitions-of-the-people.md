Compare three central outcomes without assuming they were interchangeable. The American Revolution established independence and a constitutional republic while leaving chattel slavery and Indigenous dispossession embedded in the wider political order. The French Revolution abolished many legal privileges, repeatedly changed its form of government, and spread new claims about national sovereignty while experiencing exclusion and severe repression. The Haitian Revolution overthrew slavery in a major plantation colony and established a new independent state, even as its leaders struggled over labor and authority.

Each case invites a different test of the phrase 'the people.' In America, political representation grew out of colonial institutions whose electorate was restricted and whose territory included Indigenous nations. In France, claiming that sovereignty resided in the nation challenged monarchical authority but did not settle gender and property qualifications. In Haiti, formerly enslaved people made freedom from ownership central to a new political community, although full social and economic equality remained difficult to achieve.

Use this comparison as an analytical tool, not a ranking. Which revolution most directly changed a person's status under slavery? Which created a durable constitutional framework? Which most dramatically attacked hereditary legal privilege? These are different questions. A responsible historical judgment identifies the criterion before declaring a 'winner.'
