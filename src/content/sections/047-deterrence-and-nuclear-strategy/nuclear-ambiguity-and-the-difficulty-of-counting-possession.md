Not every government publicly describes its nuclear status in the same manner. Israel has long maintained a policy of ambiguity regarding nuclear weapons and is widely assessed by independent researchers to possess them. Other states publish limited information, and the definitions used in official reports may differ. Public estimates of possession are therefore not necessarily official declarations by the governments concerned.

This is a good application of the lessons from Chapter 6. An estimate may be carefully researched without being exact. Independent researchers examine available public information and clearly label their assumptions and uncertainties. A table containing a number for every state does not establish that each number is known with the same confidence. Some figures may be derived from official disclosures; others are inferred. Readers should avoid converting estimates into an impression of perfect measurement.

Ambiguity is also a diplomatic condition. A lack of public confirmation can allow different audiences to maintain different understandings of a political relationship, but it may also make transparency and public accountability more difficult. In discussions of regional security, the absence of an official statement is not the same as the absence of a capability. Equally, a state's peaceful nuclear activities do not prove that it possesses nuclear weapons. A serious analysis must distinguish established legal commitments, public policies, independent assessments and allegations.
