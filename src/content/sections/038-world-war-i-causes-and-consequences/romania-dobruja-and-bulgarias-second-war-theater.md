Romania entered the war on the Allied side in August 1916, hoping to gain territories from Austria-Hungary, particularly Transylvania. Central Powers armies responded by attacking from several directions. Bulgaria fought in the Dobruja region alongside German and Ottoman forces. Major actions included the capture of Tutrakan (Turtucaia), and Romania's position deteriorated rapidly despite resistance in several theaters. The Romanian government and military later continued fighting from Moldavia, with the situation transformed by Russia's 1917 revolution.

Southern Dobruja had been transferred from Bulgaria to Romania after the Second Balkan War of 1913; wartime operations reopened that dispute. Military occupation, treaties signed under pressure and the eventual postwar settlement were not the same thing. Do not claim that a temporary wartime transfer ended the question permanently. The region included Bulgarian, Romanian, Turkish, Tatar and other communities, and its inhabitants' choices were more varied than national maps imply.
