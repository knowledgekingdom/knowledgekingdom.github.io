Austria-Hungary's leaders debated their response after Sarajevo and obtained German assurances of strong support, commonly called the 'blank cheque.' They aimed to punish or weaken Serbia and feared that apparent passivity would encourage nationalist movements inside the empire. On 23 July Vienna delivered a severe ultimatum giving Serbia forty-eight hours to respond. Among its demands were measures against hostile agitation and a role for Austro-Hungarian representatives in investigations and repression inside Serbia. Belgrade accepted much of the text but qualified or rejected provisions regarded as violating its sovereignty.

Austria-Hungary declared war on 28 July. Russia moved toward military intervention; Germany issued ultimatums, declared war on Russia on 1 August and France on 3 August, and invaded neutral Belgium on 4 August. Britain's declaration of war against Germany on 4 August reflected several concerns, including Belgian neutrality and the danger of German dominance on the continent. The exact sequence matters: an assassination did not cause a universal declaration on one day.
