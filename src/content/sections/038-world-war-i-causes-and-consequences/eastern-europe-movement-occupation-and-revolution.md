The Eastern Front covered vast distances and involved different conditions from Belgium and northern France. Germany, Austria-Hungary and Russia fought over territory from the Baltic to the Black Sea. The 1915 Central Powers offensive pushed Russian armies eastward, creating a crisis of supply and confidence within the Russian Empire. In 1916 the Brusilov Offensive inflicted severe losses on Austria-Hungary and influenced the wider military balance, but Russian gains did not solve the empire's economic and political problems.

Military defeat interacted with food shortages, labor disputes and distrust of the monarchy. Revolution in Russia in 1917 overthrew the tsar, and a second revolution brought the Bolsheviks to power. They sought to leave the war and signed the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk with the Central Powers in March 1918, surrendering control of vast territories under severe terms. The end of Russia's participation freed German forces for transfer westward, but it also created political uncertainty and gave independence movements a chance to act.
