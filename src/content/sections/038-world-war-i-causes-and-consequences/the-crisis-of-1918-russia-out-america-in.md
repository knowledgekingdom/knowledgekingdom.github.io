In spring 1918 Germany launched major offensives in France, hoping to win before the growing American presence became decisive. The temporary withdrawal of Russia had released divisions for the western campaign. German forces initially advanced, but they could not convert tactical successes into a sustainable victory. Allied armies improved coordination under unified leadership and mounted increasingly effective counteroffensives. German manpower and supply conditions worsened as Allied resources expanded; the maritime blockade and cumulative losses contributed to the deterioration.

The outcome was not inevitable from the first American landing or the signing of Brest-Litovsk. There were contingent decisions, tactical adaptations, morale crises and varying local results. German leaders also faced the collapse of their partners. A map showing only the Western Front can obscure the order in which the Central Powers' political structure came apart.
