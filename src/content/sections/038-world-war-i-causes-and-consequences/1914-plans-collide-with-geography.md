German operations in the west crossed Belgium to move against France, provoking fierce Belgian resistance and British intervention. The German army advanced deeply into France but failed to win a rapid decisive victory. French and British forces counterattacked at the First Battle of the Marne in September. Armies then maneuvered toward the North Sea in an effort to outflank each other; the result was an extended front of fortified positions. Civilian populations in occupied territories suffered killings, destruction, forced requisition and displacement, and claims in wartime propaganda must be checked against contemporary documentation.

In the east, Russia attacked toward East Prussia and Galicia. Germany defeated Russian forces at Tannenberg in August 1914, while Austria-Hungary faced serious setbacks. Serbia repelled Austro-Hungarian offensives that year but endured immense losses and epidemic hardship. These movements show why the Western Front's trench stalemate must not be mistaken for the whole war.
