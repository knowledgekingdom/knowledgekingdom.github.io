Bulgaria attacked Serbia in October 1915 after reaching agreements with the Central Powers. Coordinated offensives by German and Austro-Hungarian forces from the north and Bulgarian armies from the east overwhelmed Serbia's defense. Serbian military forces and many civilians retreated under disastrous conditions through Montenegro and Albania toward the Adriatic, where Allied evacuation and later reorganization enabled surviving troops to continue fighting. The conquest changed the military geography of southeastern Europe and improved overland connections between the Central Powers and the Ottoman Empire.

Bulgarian forces occupied areas including Vardar Macedonia, where different communities perceived the new authority in sharply different ways. Supporters viewed the campaign as national reunification; others experienced imposed rule, requisitions, changing administration and coercion. Historians must examine both military achievement and the lives of civilians subject to occupation. The campaign did not deliver an uncomplicated or durable settlement.
