<strong>c. 5500–4000 BCE:</strong> Ubaid traditions and expanding settlement networks in southern Mesopotamia; dates and regional definitions vary.

<strong>c. 4000–3100 BCE:</strong> Uruk period; major urban growth and new administrative practices in the south.

<strong>c. 3300–3000 BCE:</strong> Early proto-cuneiform accounts and related developments in writing, with scholarly debates over stages and origins.

<strong>c. 2900–2350 BCE:</strong> Early Dynastic city-states, competition and increasingly prominent royal institutions.

<strong>c. 2350–2150 BCE:</strong> Akkadian dynasty and regional imperial rule; dates approximate.

<strong>c. 2112–2004 BCE:</strong> Third Dynasty of Ur and its extensive archives.

<strong>c. 1792–1750 BCE:</strong> Reign of Hammurabi in a commonly used middle chronology; the famous legal stele belongs to this broad period.

Dates in very early history depend on archaeological periodization, later king lists, and choices among chronological models. Treat them as useful anchors rather than exact timestamps for every development.
