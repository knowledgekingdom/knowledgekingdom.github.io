1\. <strong>Probability is a description of uncertainty</strong>, not a guarantee of what happens in one case.

2\. The numerator tells you how many; <strong>the denominator tells you out of whom or what</strong>.

3\. An absolute change in percentage points differs from a relative percentage change.

4\. Multiplying probabilities requires an appropriate independence assumption; mutually exclusive events are different.

5\. <strong>P(A \| B) need not equal P(B \| A)</strong>. Always check which condition the question states.

6\. The usefulness of a warning signal depends on the <strong>base rate</strong> as well as the system's performance.

7\. <strong>Expected value</strong> summarizes weighted outcomes but does not guarantee a personal result or capture all preferences.

8\. The shape and spread of outcomes may matter as much as the mean, particularly for rare serious harms.

9\. A streak does not prove a pattern, and a larger sample cannot repair systematic selection bias.

10\. Forecasts should be evaluated over many comparable events for <strong>calibration</strong> and usefulness.

11\. Compounding makes repeated changes multiplicative; equal percentage falls and rises do not cancel.

12\. Sensible risk management weighs consequences, uncertainty, resilience, and fairness—not just average money.
