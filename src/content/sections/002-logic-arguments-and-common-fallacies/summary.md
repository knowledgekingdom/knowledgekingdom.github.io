<strong>The central distinction:</strong> A conclusion may be true even when the offered argument is weak. A formally valid inference may still rely on false premises. Evaluate both the evidence and the path from evidence to conclusion.

<strong>The five-move habit:</strong> Reconstruct the claim; classify the reasoning; test premises; test the inference; steelman the objection and revise your conclusion if needed.

<strong>Essential terms:</strong> Argument, premise, conclusion, proposition, deductive validity, soundness, modus ponens, modus tollens, affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent, necessary condition, sufficient condition, induction, abduction, counterexample, straw man, steel man, ad hominem, false dilemma, slippery slope, circular reasoning, hasty generalization, selection bias, burden of proof, fallacy fallacy.
