Monsoon: seasonally recurring wind and rainfall patterns that shaped the timing and risks of sailing. Entrepôt: a port or commercial center where goods are collected, stored and traded onward. Diaspora: a community with enduring links across more than one place, though identities and relationships vary. Cartaz: a Portuguese maritime pass system applied with varying reach and enforcement. Estado da Índia: the Portuguese network of Asian settlements, forts and offices, not a state governing the entire subcontinent.

Chokepoint: a passage whose geography offers potential control over movement, though effective power depends on enforcement and alternatives. Merchant credit: arrangements that finance trade before a final sale or settlement. Monopoly: exclusive supply or trading rights that may be claimed in law yet imperfectly enforced. Tribute: a broad label for asymmetrical transfers and diplomatic claims, whose meaning must be checked in each context. Connected history: a way of explaining how interactions among regions shaped their development without assuming that any one civilization supplied every cause.

The central question to take away is this: when a new empire arrives at a trading port, what does it actually acquire—land, fortifications, ships, contracts, local cooperation, tax authority, and the ability to enforce its claims? These forms of power overlap but are not identical.
