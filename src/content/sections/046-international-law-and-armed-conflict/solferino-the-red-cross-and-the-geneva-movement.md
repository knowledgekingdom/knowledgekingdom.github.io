In 1859 Henry Dunant witnessed the suffering associated with the Battle of Solferino. His subsequent book, A Memory of Solferino, helped inspire a movement to organize voluntary relief and protect wounded soldiers. A committee formed in Geneva in 1863, and an international convention followed in 1864. The institution later known as the International Committee of the Red Cross became an important participant in the development of humanitarian law. \[<a href="/046-international-law-and-armed-conflict/sources/#source-10" class="cite">10</a>\]

The significance of this story is not that no one previously cared for wounded people. Rather, private moral initiative was transformed into a recurring institutional arrangement. Humanitarian care could be prepared in peacetime, recognized by governments and made less dependent upon the goodwill of a particular commander. Neutrality could create access across opposing lines; impartiality could ensure that the basis for aid was need rather than allegiance.

The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement includes the ICRC, the International Federation and national societies. Their roles differ. The ICRC has a particular mandate in armed conflict, whereas the Federation coordinates support in many disasters and emergencies. A humanitarian emblem is not simply a charitable logo: in relevant circumstances it communicates legally protected status. Misuse can undermine trust in people providing aid. \[<a href="/046-international-law-and-armed-conflict/sources/#source-10" class="cite">10</a>\]\[<a href="/046-international-law-and-armed-conflict/sources/#source-11" class="cite">11</a>\]\[<a href="/046-international-law-and-armed-conflict/sources/#source-55" class="cite">55</a>\]
