In ordinary language, an aggressive country might mean a government that threatens neighbors or acts arrogantly. In international law, aggression concerns unlawful interstate armed force of a particular character. General Assembly Resolution 3314 (1974) provides a definition and examples for the international institutional framework. Not every serious political grievance establishes aggression in this sense. \[<a href="/046-international-law-and-armed-conflict/sources/#source-19" class="cite">19</a>\]

The crime of aggression under the Rome Statute is narrower than a general conclusion that a state violated the Charter. It is directed at persons in a position effectively to control or direct a state's political or military action, and requires a manifest Charter violation judged by its character, gravity and scale. The ICC's jurisdiction over this crime has special limitations that differ from its jurisdiction over the other core crimes. \[<a href="/046-international-law-and-armed-conflict/sources/#source-20" class="cite">20</a>\]

In March 2022 the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution ES-11/1 condemning Russia's aggression against Ukraine and demanding withdrawal. This is a significant official expression of the Assembly's position, not an individual criminal conviction. The legal evaluation of interstate conduct can be well supported without establishing that every person associated with a government's decision meets the elements and jurisdictional conditions for a particular crime. \[<a href="/046-international-law-and-armed-conflict/sources/#source-21" class="cite">21</a>\] Avoid replacing the actual legal document with a headline which either overstates or dismisses its significance.
