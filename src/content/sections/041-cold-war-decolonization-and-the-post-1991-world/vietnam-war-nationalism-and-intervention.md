The Vietnam War emerged from colonial conflict, Cold War strategic assumptions and competing Vietnamese political projects. North Vietnam and the National Liberation Front fought against South Vietnam and its American supporters. The United States escalated combat deployments and air operations in the 1960s, arguing that communist victory might destabilize other regional governments. For many Vietnamese, however, the war was also a struggle over national independence, political control and social order.

Mass bombing, chemical defoliants, village destruction, massacres and displacement had devastating civilian consequences. North Vietnamese and communist forces also committed killings and coercive acts. Public opposition to the war rose in the United States and other countries as costs and doubts about progress mounted. American combat forces withdrew after the 1973 Paris agreements; Saigon fell in 1975, and Vietnam was unified under communist rule. The conflict demonstrated limits to overwhelming military resources when political legitimacy, local institutions, external sanctuary and adversaries' persistence remain contested.
