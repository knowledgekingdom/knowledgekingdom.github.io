Socialist Yugoslavia had occupied a distinctive position outside the Soviet bloc, but its federal structure, economic difficulties and nationalist polarization became destabilizing after the death of Josip Broz Tito in 1980. The breakup of the federation in the 1990s involved wars in Croatia and Bosnia and Herzegovina, mass expulsions and atrocities. The Srebrenica genocide in 1995 and the siege of Sarajevo are among the central events that demand clear historical description and careful attribution of responsibility.

The Kosovo conflict later brought further displacement, abuses and a 1999 NATO air campaign against the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia. Disputes over sovereignty and international intervention continued afterward. These wars warn against a simplistic belief that communism's collapse inevitably produced peace or that ancient hatreds alone explain violence. Political leadership, propaganda, institutions, military organization and international choices mattered. Bulgaria's geographic proximity made regional insecurity, refugee movements and new diplomatic priorities especially relevant to its transition.
