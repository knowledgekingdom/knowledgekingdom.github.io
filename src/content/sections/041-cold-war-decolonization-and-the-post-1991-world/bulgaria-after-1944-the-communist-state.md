In September 1944 Bulgaria changed wartime alignment amid Soviet military entry and a Fatherland Front takeover. In the following years communist leaders consolidated authority through courts, repression, political exclusion and institutional restructuring. The monarchy ended after a 1946 referendum, and Bulgaria became a people's republic. Industry and banking were nationalized, agriculture was reorganized through collectivization, and the government expanded state planning. Bulgaria entered Soviet-led economic and military institutions and became closely tied to Moscow.

The communist decades brought industrial jobs, mass education, urbanization, electrification and greater access to certain public services. They also involved limited freedom of expression, political imprisonment, censorship, surveillance, restrictions on private enterprise and constraints on religious institutions. The comparison needs care: improvements in literacy or infrastructure are not evidence that repression did not exist; accounts of coercion do not mean every factory, school or household experience was identical. Personal memories differ because citizens occupied different social positions and experienced different periods of the regime.
