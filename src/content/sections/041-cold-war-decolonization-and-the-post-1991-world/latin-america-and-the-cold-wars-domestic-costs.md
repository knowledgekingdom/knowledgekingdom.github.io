Many Latin American societies experienced the Cold War through struggles over land, inequality, political representation and the military's role in government. The 1954 overthrow of Guatemala's elected government involved U.S. covert action amid concerns about communist influence and the interests of American companies. In Chile, Salvador Allende's elected socialist government faced severe polarization and economic conflict; General Augusto Pinochet's 1973 coup inaugurated a dictatorship marked by torture, disappearances and exile. Other countries experienced guerrilla campaigns, military coups and international aid to authoritarian security forces.

The region also saw efforts at democratic restoration, human-rights documentation and later transitional justice. No single narrative of leftist idealism or anticommunist security captures all cases. Some insurgent movements used coercion and attacked civilians; some governments institutionalized state terror on a much greater scale. Evidence should distinguish actors, time periods and responsibility. The lesson for geopolitics is uncomfortable: foreign powers often treated political order and strategic loyalty as more urgent than the civil rights they otherwise professed to defend.
