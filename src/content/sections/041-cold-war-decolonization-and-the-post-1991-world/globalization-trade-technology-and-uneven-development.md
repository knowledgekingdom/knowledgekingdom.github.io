After 1991 expanding trade, digital networks, container shipping and multinational production connected many economies more tightly. China's reform and opening, begun in the late 1970s and transformed by later investment and export expansion, became central to global manufacturing. India undertook major economic reforms in 1991 and developed important information-technology and service industries. Other Asian, African and Latin American economies followed varied pathways; some gained new opportunities while others remained dependent on volatile commodities or carried heavy debt burdens.

Global extreme poverty declined substantially over recent decades, although estimates depend on changing price thresholds, survey methods and the period measured. Gains have been geographically uneven, and within-country inequality can rise even while average living standards improve. Globalization is therefore not a single policy choice with identical effects everywhere. Cheap consumer goods, remittances and knowledge exchange coexist with labor exploitation, environmental damage, vulnerability to supply disruptions and political demands to protect domestic industries. Chapters on economics will examine those mechanisms in more detail.
