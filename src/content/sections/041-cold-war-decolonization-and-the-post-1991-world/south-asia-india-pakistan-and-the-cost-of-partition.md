British India became independent in August 1947, divided into India and Pakistan through a process of partition shaped by colonial policy, constitutional disputes, religious identity and competing political visions. Leaders such as Mohandas Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru and Muhammad Ali Jinnah pursued different understandings of representation and nationhood. The rushed demarcation of borders, political mobilization and communal violence helped produce one of the twentieth century's largest episodes of forced migration. Estimates of deaths vary widely, making precise totals a source problem rather than an excuse to minimize suffering.

India established a republic with a written constitution and periodic elections, but faced enormous inequalities, regional demands and the challenge of integrating princely states. Pakistan experienced repeated military intervention and unresolved disputes over national identity and provincial representation. East Pakistan became Bangladesh in 1971 after a devastating war marked by atrocities and mass displacement. The Kashmir dispute became a continuing source of India-Pakistan conflict. South Asian decolonization therefore created states and democratic possibilities while also leaving deep problems of borders, security and citizenship.
