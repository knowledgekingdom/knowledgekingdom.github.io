Following Soviet dissolution, the United States retained unmatched global military reach for a time, and commentators sometimes described a 'unipolar moment'. NATO operations in the Balkans, interventions in Afghanistan after 2001, and the 2003 invasion of Iraq raised questions about security, sovereignty, terrorism, intelligence and international law. The intervention in Afghanistan overthrew the Taliban government in 2001, but prolonged efforts to build a stable state failed; the Taliban regained power in 2021 after international withdrawal. Iraq's war and occupation brought major instability and suffering.

At the same time, China grew more influential, Russia sought to restore military and geopolitical power, and regional governments developed independent strategies. The annexation of Crimea by Russia in 2014 and its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 demonstrated that territorial conquest remained a major European security issue. International institutions provided forums, humanitarian assistance and legal principles, but they could not ensure enforcement in every case. The post-Cold War world was never simply 'the end of history'.
