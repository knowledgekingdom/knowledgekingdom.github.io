South Africa's apartheid system institutionalized racial segregation and political exclusion, generating internal resistance, harsh repression and international campaigns. Anti-apartheid activists used strikes, legal challenges, political organizing, armed struggle and global boycotts, with differing strategies and disagreements. In neighboring Namibia, independence movements fought South African rule until a negotiated transition brought independence in 1990. Zimbabwe became independent in 1980 after a protracted conflict under white-minority rule.

Angola's 1975 independence from Portugal was followed by civil war involving local political movements, Cuba, South Africa, the Soviet Union and the United States. The conflict's international dimensions were substantial, but Angolan parties were not merely puppets. Diplomacy, regional pressures and changing domestic balances eventually reshaped these wars. In South Africa the negotiated transition led to universal national elections in 1994 and Nelson Mandela's presidency, but inequality and the legacies of dispossession did not disappear at the ballot box. Political freedom, reconciliation and material justice remained connected but distinct tasks.
