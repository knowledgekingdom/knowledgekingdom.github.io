In August 1990 Iraqi forces invaded and occupied Kuwait. Baghdad's government advanced complaints concerning oil production, debts and territory, but the decision to seize a sovereign neighbor was an act of aggression. The invasion altered regional security and threatened important economic interests. The United Nations Security Council condemned the occupation, imposed sanctions and ultimately authorized member states to use force under specified conditions if Iraq did not withdraw. A U.S.-led coalition expelled Iraqi forces in early 1991. \[<a href="/042-why-wars-begin/sources/#source-15" class="cite">15</a>\]

This episode helps separate motives, bargaining and legality. Financial disputes and contested borders were part of the political background, but neither made conquest lawful. The Iraqi government miscalculated international reaction and the scale of military opposition. The coalition's initial objective of expelling Iraqi forces was distinct from the later question of how Iraq should be governed. Analysts should compare a government's stated reasons with its chosen methods and the diplomatic alternatives available, then evaluate why other actors decided to enforce the international boundary.
