The story we have followed begins with people, natural resources, skills and wealth. It proceeds through recruitment, education, institutions, public budgets and industrial activity. It continues through the less visible activities of provision, maintenance, transport, communication and care. At every stage, something can be misunderstood if we count assets without examining institutions. Finally, military capabilities must be related to political objectives and the choices of other actors.

Logistics is central because it reveals the material foundations of power. Yet logistics alone is not destiny. An institution may be well supplied and given an incoherent mission; a government may possess excellent equipment while lacking public legitimacy; an alliance may share common values while struggling to coordinate practical responsibilities. The strongest explanation is usually one that identifies how several mechanisms interact in a particular historical setting.

If you remember one sentence from this chapter, remember this: military power is organised, sustained and politically directed capacity, not merely a collection of people and machines. The next chapter turns to diplomacy, alliances and the balance of power, where governments seek security, influence and cooperation without relying exclusively on military means. The institutions described here matter most when they are understood as part of that larger political world.
