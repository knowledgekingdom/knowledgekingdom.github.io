Readiness concerns whether an institution can perform its assigned tasks at the required standard and time. Depending on the reporting system, measures may include personnel, training, equipment condition and other resources. A readiness statement is always conditional on what has been measured and how the requirement was defined.

A machine may be physically functional while lacking the qualified operator necessary to use it. A trained specialist may be available while the relevant equipment is being inspected. An organisation may be ready for one task but not another. Aggregating these different dimensions into one simple percentage may hide rather than clarify the nature of the problem.

Readiness is also dynamic. An organisation that postpones maintenance may report greater immediate availability while creating larger obligations in the future. A training programme may temporarily remove personnel from ordinary duties while improving long-term competence. This is the type of intertemporal trade-off that Chapter 7 examined through feedback loops.
