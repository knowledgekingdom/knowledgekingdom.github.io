A commander is responsible for decisions within an assigned sphere of authority. Staff organisations support decision-making through administration, planning, personnel management, legal advice, information assessment and coordination. The distinction resembles that between a hospital director, clinical departments and administrative specialists: the director cannot personally perform all the tasks for which the institution is responsible.

Hierarchy can help establish who is accountable and which instructions take precedence. It can also create difficulties. Subordinates may be reluctant to communicate unwelcome information, especially where their career prospects depend on flattering senior officials. A leader who hears only what people think the leader wishes to hear is not exercising meaningful control, however extensive the hierarchy appears. The quality of reporting is as important as the appearance of obedience.

An institution must distinguish obedience to lawful authority from personal loyalty to a particular officeholder. Professional responsibility includes knowing legal constraints and providing accurate advice even when it is uncomfortable. Where institutions obscure responsibility or discourage scrutiny, failures may be attributed solely to the lowest-ranking people. The resulting explanations can protect senior reputations while leaving the underlying problem unchanged. \[<a href="/044-military-organization-and-logistics/sources/#source-4" class="cite">4</a>\]
