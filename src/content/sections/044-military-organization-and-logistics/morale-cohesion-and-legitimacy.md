Morale is an individual's or group's psychological disposition towards its circumstances and responsibilities. Cohesion describes the bonds that allow members of an organisation to function together. Neither can be read directly from the number of people wearing uniforms. Shared experience, perceived competence, leadership, reliable pay, fair treatment, trust in colleagues and the legitimacy of the institution can all influence them.

One person may remain committed to colleagues while doubting the political purpose of a war. Another may support the government's objective yet feel alienated by an abusive superior. These are different dimensions of morale. A military institution that treats every expression of doubt as disloyalty loses valuable information about how its members experience their obligations.

Historical observers sometimes attribute defeat to the supposed moral character of a whole nation. Such explanations replace evidence with stereotypes. Desertion, exhaustion and refusal to serve may reflect conditions specific to a time and institution. The same society can experience intense mobilisation in one conflict and widespread opposition in another. Study how military personnel were recruited, treated and represented before drawing conclusions about their motives.
