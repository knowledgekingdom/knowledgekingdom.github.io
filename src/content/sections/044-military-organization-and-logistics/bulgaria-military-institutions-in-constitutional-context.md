Bulgaria's geographical position connects Southeastern Europe, the Black Sea and wider European transport networks. Its modern military history is also shaped by the formation of the Bulgarian state after the Russo-Turkish War, two world wars, the Cold War and the country's transition to parliamentary democracy. The Ministry of Defence traces the formation of the Bulgarian Territorial Army to July 1878. \[<a href="/044-military-organization-and-logistics/sources/#source-30" class="cite">30</a>\]

Under the Constitution of the Republic of Bulgaria, the armed forces have responsibilities related to the country's sovereignty, security, independence and territorial integrity. The President is designated Supreme Commander-in-Chief; other constitutional provisions allocate important responsibilities to the National Assembly and Council of Ministers. This division matters because a single title does not describe the entire system of lawful authority and accountability. \[<a href="/044-military-organization-and-logistics/sources/#source-5" class="cite">5</a>\]

Bulgaria formally joined NATO on 29 March 2004. Membership connects Bulgarian defence institutions to a collective political and institutional framework while national democratic responsibilities remain important. \[<a href="/044-military-organization-and-logistics/sources/#source-31" class="cite">31</a>\] For a small country, the debate about defence cannot be reduced to comparisons of the largest states' inventories. Education, public finance, the condition of civilian infrastructure, administrative competence, international cooperation and the country's actual security obligations all deserve attention. A responsible assessment requires evidence rather than assumptions about national character.
