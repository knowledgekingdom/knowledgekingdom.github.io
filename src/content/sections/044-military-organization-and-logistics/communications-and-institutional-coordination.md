Communication systems transmit information; institutions determine what information means and who is authorised to act upon it. A communication network can be technically sophisticated yet fail to produce coherent decisions if organisations disagree about terminology, authority or responsibilities. The problem is social as well as technological.

Consider a multilingual international rescue mission. Several organisations may possess reliable radios and databases, but if the same word has different meanings in their procedures, their reports may be misunderstood. The same problem can occur inside one government when agencies use incompatible record systems. Common definitions, education and professional relationships help make information usable.

New digital systems also create dependencies on software, electricity, data quality and specialist labour. They can support more extensive record keeping, but a precise digital display may still contain inaccurate information. The danger is misplaced confidence in the apparent completeness of a screen. As with all evidence, the source, age, purpose and uncertainty of the information must be understood. This principle connects military organisation directly to the later chapters on computers, data and artificial intelligence.
