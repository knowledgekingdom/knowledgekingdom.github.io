An armed force is a public institution, or a formally constituted military organisation, with personnel, authority, rules and material resources assigned to military functions. Most states maintain a land force, a navy if their circumstances require one, and an air force or air component. Some also organise specialised cyber, space, coastguard or other services. The boundaries between defence institutions, police and paramilitary organisations differ considerably among countries.

The land forces are concerned with activities on land; navies deal with maritime environments; air forces concern the air domain. Each is supported by people whose work is much less visible than the dramatic images normally associated with a military: doctors, engineers, cooks, mechanics, accountants, administrators, instructors, legal advisers and communications specialists. A modern armed force also relies on civilian employees and suppliers.

Names alone are misleading. The word army can refer specifically to a land service, or informally to all the armed forces. A coastguard may be military in one country and civilian in another. Internal security bodies may be legally separate from the armed forces or have overlapping duties. When comparing countries, first establish their institutional definitions. A statistical total that combines all security personnel in one country but only active-duty military personnel in another cannot support a meaningful direct comparison.
