<strong>Core idea.</strong> Human evolution is a long, branching history centered initially in Africa. Early hominins developed upright walking and tool use long before modern humans. Different human lineages occupied the planet simultaneously and sometimes interbred. The worldwide spread of Homo sapiens happened through multiple, uneven population movements. Archaeological dates are evidence-based estimates with limits, not a permanent list of firsts.

<strong>Essential terms.</strong> Hominin; common ancestor; natural selection; genetic drift; bipedalism; Australopithecus; Homo erectus; Homo sapiens; fossil record; stratigraphy; radiocarbon dating; luminescence dating; Oldowan; Acheulean; introgression; Neanderthal; Denisovan; paleogeography; Sahul; Sunda; Beringia; Last Glacial Maximum; cumulative culture; archaeology; population dispersal.

<strong>Anchor dates worth retaining.</strong> About 3.6 million years ago: Laetoli bipedal footprints. About 3.3 million: Lomekwi tools. Around 1.8 million: early Homo beyond Africa. Around 300,000: early Homo sapiens evidence. Around 50,000 or earlier: late Pleistocene people in Sahul; exact earliest occupation debated. About 21,000–23,000: White Sands footprints if supported dating is accepted. Around 12,000 years ago and afterward: major transitions toward food production in several regions. Dates are approximate and not equally secure.
