<strong>Misconception 1: Humans evolved directly from today's monkeys or chimpanzees.</strong> Humans and other living apes share older ancestors. The living species are relatives on different branches. A modern chimpanzee is no more a living human ancestor than a cousin is your grandparent.

<strong>Misconception 2: Evolution moved in a straight line from 'primitive' to 'advanced'.</strong> Different hominin lineages overlapped and sometimes mixed. Traits appeared in different combinations and at different times. Evolutionary history contains branches, dead ends, and changing environments, not guaranteed progress toward ourselves.

<strong>Misconception 3: The first toolmaker must have been Homo sapiens.</strong> Many stone tools are much older than our species. The Lomekwi artifacts predate it by millions of years. Ancient technologies cannot automatically be assigned to a modern human inventor.

<strong>Misconception 4: One expedition left Africa and populated the world.</strong> Population movements happened across many generations and included earlier and later dispersals, local extinctions, return movements, and contact with other hominins. Straight arrows on maps are shorthand for research hypotheses, not literal travel itineraries.

<strong>Misconception 5: Prehistoric people were intellectually empty until farming or writing.</strong> People used tools, navigated complex environments, built social relationships, conveyed knowledge, and made images long before cities appeared. Writing changed the preservation and administration of information; it did not create intelligence from nothing.

A related caution concerns the term <strong>race</strong>. Human genetic diversity is real, but conventional racial categories do not represent ancient, isolated, biologically pure branches of humanity. Variation is distributed across and within populations, and ancestry reflects repeated movement and mixture. Biological information about populations should be handled carefully; the history of human migration does not endorse claims of group superiority or any hierarchy of human dignity.
