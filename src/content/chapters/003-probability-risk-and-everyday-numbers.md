At 8:17 on a busy Monday morning, the manager of an online store sees an urgent red notification. A payment has been marked as suspicious. Her software provider proudly advertises a 90 percent detection rate. “Ninety percent accurate,” she thinks, glancing at the name on the order. “That probably means this customer is a fraudster.” She is about to cancel the transaction and send an accusatory message. But a colleague asks an awkward question: “How many honest transactions get flagged?” The answer is five percent.

The manager is puzzled. Ninety percent sounds close to certainty. Five percent sounds small. Yet most transactions are honest, and five percent of a very large honest group can produce more alerts than 90 percent of a very small fraudulent group. The software may be useful while an individual alert is still far from proof. Its <em>true-positive rate</em> describes what happens <strong>if a transaction is fraudulent</strong>. The manager wants to know what is true <strong>given that a transaction was flagged</strong>. Those two questions reverse the condition; they need not have the same answer.

A mistaken response here is not simply a bad calculation. It can damage a customer's trust, waste staff time, or cause a business to reject legitimate orders. Exactly the same confusion appears when people discuss medical screening, crime predictions, election forecasts, security alarms, and rare scientific signals. A number may be technically correct and still answer the wrong question. This is why we need <strong>probability literacy</strong>: the ability to understand uncertain claims, ask the right questions about them, and act without pretending that uncertainty has disappeared.

This chapter is not intended to make you a professional statistician. Its aim is more practical and, in some ways, more demanding. By the end, you should be able to reconstruct a percentage from counts, calculate the meaning of a warning signal, distinguish expected gain from unacceptable loss, interpret a forecast, recognize an overconfident conclusion from a small sample, and make a decision when there is no risk-free choice. The mathematics will stay accessible. Every important calculation is explained in words as well as numbers.
